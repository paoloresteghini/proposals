01 · The problem

Strong work, a site that feels dead

You told us the site is hard to navigate, the main calls to action get lost, and the design feels out of date. The analytics back up all three.

01

A menu doing too many jobs

The main menu holds 22 links. Issues, content types, programs and a single tag all sit side by side, so Pensions, Press Releases, Campus First and State Unions look like the same kind of thing. Readers have to learn your structure before they can find anything.

02

The asks are buried, and nobody can see if they work

Readers finish an article and leave: each US visitor views 1.7 pages on average. Around 2,700 visits a year start on the donate page and last 12 seconds on average. The site does not record a single donation, newsletter signup or event registration, so there is no way to tell what is working.

03

The look and the loose ends

The design reads older than the organization. Small cracks add to it: 1 in 9 US visitors hits a "Page not found", and every site search shows "You searched for %%searchphrase%%" in the browser tab and in Google.

What is not broken

The reporting. Your strongest stories hold readers for over a minute and twenty seconds, and CT Capitol Report sends you 17,000 visits a year with the highest engagement of any source. People come for the work. The redesign makes sure they stay, go deeper, and act.

03 · Performance

Twenty-three seconds before a phone can use it

A redesign that only changes the look leaves the slowest part of the site in place. Google treats a mobile page as good when its main content arrives within 2.5 seconds. Your homepage today:

22.8s

before the homepage is usable on a phone. For most of that time it looks loaded but will not respond.

9.6s

before the first words appear. This is the moment a reader decides whether to wait.

26

other companies the page contacts before it settles, most of them advertising and tracking networks.

53

out of 100 on Google's mobile performance score. 154 files and 1.9 MB for a single page.

None of it was reckless. Each plugin, pixel and widget arrived to solve a real problem on the day it was installed. The rebuild keeps what earns its place, such as analytics and your donation platform, and removes the rest.

04 · Scope & deliverables

A full redesign, built in Elementor

Delivered end to end by one person: strategy, design and build. Your WordPress, your content and your publishing workflow stay where they are.

Navigation & IA PRIORITY

A new sitemap and menu that separate issues, content types and programs, so every visitor can find their way in two clicks.

Calls to action PRIORITY

Donate, subscribe and attend, placed where readers are ready for them: at the end of every article, on issue pages and across the homepage.

Performance PRIORITY

Speed is a core goal, not a finishing touch. A lean build, fewer third parties, optimized images, measured against Core Web Vitals before and after.

Visual redesign

New type, color, layout and imagery treatment that make the site feel current and active, applied everywhere.

Elementor theme build

A clean Elementor build replacing the current Themify setup, with templates your team can edit without a developer.

Content templates

Purpose-built layouts for the homepage, articles, research, issue hubs, events and donate, plus listing pages.

Search & broken links

Working on-site search with filters by issue and type, and redirects for the old addresses real readers still reach.

Measurement

Tracking for donations, signups, registrations and key clicks, with bot traffic filtered out, so you can see what the new site delivers.

SEO, AEO & accessibility

Structure that helps Google and AI answer engines find and cite your work, and a WCAG-minded accessibility pass.

Launch & handover

QA, deploy and a walkthrough so your team owns the new site with confidence.

05 · Approach

Structure first, surface second

We fix the map before we paint the rooms. The navigation, the calls to action and what gets measured are settled and signed off first, so every design and build decision after that serves a structure we already agree on.

06 · Timeline · roughly 10 to 12 weeks

WK 1-2

Discovery & structure

Audit the site and analytics, map the new sitemap and menu, and agree where each call to action lives. Clean up tracking so we have an honest baseline. You sign off the structure before design begins.

WK 3-6

Design

Set the new visual direction on the homepage, then design each template: articles, research, issue hubs, events, donate and listings.

WK 7-11

Elementor build

Build the approved designs in Elementor on your existing WordPress, rebuild the key pages, and set up redirects, search and measurement. Speed is tested on every template as it is built, not at the end, alongside SEO, accessibility and QA.

WK 12

Launch

Final checks, a before and after speed report, deploy to production, and a walkthrough so your team can publish confidently from day one.

One point of contact throughout. You review at the end of each stage. No decision-by-committee, no surprises mid-build.

Number of bespoke templates

The main driver. The core set, homepage, article, research, issue hub and donate, sits near $22K. Each further unique template, for example events, staff, press or a program page such as Campus First, moves you toward $25K.

Complexity of the current setup

The site runs 55 active plugins on the Themify theme and page builder. 59 of your 71 live pages, and a share of older articles, are built in Themify and need rebuilding or converting for Elementor. Custom pieces such as the contracts database, the GDP chart and the calculator, plus event ticketing, 34 active forms and Bloomerang donations, each have to be kept, rebuilt or retired without breaking anything.

03

Re-sorting the archive

If the new structure means re-tagging older articles by issue and type, that is done by hand. The further back you want it to reach, the more it adds.
