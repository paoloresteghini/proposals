01

The page backlog is three things, which makes it fixable

Your SEO plugin is submitting a sitemap entry for every tag you've ever used (1,133) and every event you've ever held (1,157, going back to ones like a Governor Rauner day). In a 1,000 page sample of the biggest problem bucket, 461 were events, 315 were tags and 146 were recaps, and not one had ever been crawled. So this is a configuration change and a bulk tidy rather than four thousand separate decisions.

02

The decline started in January, and it isn't a penalty

You have no manual actions, and your average position actually improved, from 17.6 last July to 12.1 this July. Against the same month last year, impressions are down 42% and clicks down 22%, so it isn't only seasonal either. Your traffic sits almost entirely in definitional questions, which is the kind of search AI answers have been absorbing, though I'd want to check that properly rather than assert it.

03

One brief is carrying an enormous amount

/briefs/socialism/ alone has had 7.9m impressions and 19,672 clicks. With the Venezuela mini-brief, socialism questions account for around 60% of your search visibility. Your biggest single query is "socialism", at position 4.6 with a click-through rate of 0.12%. A very large audience is arriving at one idea.

04

Almost none of that traffic reaches what you care about

Over sixteen months, search sent 106,766 clicks to policy briefs, 5,108 to the homepage, 638 to CLER and the programs, 434 to events, 20 to membership and 13 to Donate. That's 167 times more people arriving at a brief than at CLER, around 6,673 of them every month. The briefs are doing an extraordinary job of bringing women to the site, and nothing is being asked of them once they arrive.

That last one is the biggest opportunity I've seen here. I've deliberately not put a projected number on it, because I'd only be guessing at a conversion rate until it's measured. Working out what it's actually worth is one of the things month one is for.

02 · Order of work

Order of work

Why I'd start with the measurement.

You put reporting first on your list and I'd keep it there. If we get the tracking working in the first month, everything we change after that has a before and an after to compare against. If we leave it until the end, the biggest work in the phase happens without anyone being able to see whether it helped.

It also helps to think of this as two things running alongside each other rather than one queue. The technical work doesn't need sign-off from anyone, so it can start straight away. The homepage, navigation and audience work moves at whatever pace your internal discussions and approvals run at, which is really why this is a ninety-day phase rather than a shorter one.

03 · Measurement & reporting

Measurement & reporting

Reporting you don't have to go looking for.

You asked what should be in it, what I'd build it on, and where it goes from here.

What I'd suggest: a written weekly email.

It pulls from your analytics and Search Console and arrives as plain English in an inbox every Monday. There's nothing for anyone to log into or learn. It's the smaller of the two options we talked about, and I think it's the right one for now, for the reason you gave on our call: there's nobody in-house with a digital marketing background at the moment, so what would help most is findings you can read. If you do bring someone in later, they'd inherit something with a bit of history behind it.

What would be in it

  • Who's showing real interest. Not just visits, but the sequences that matter: someone read a policy brief and then joined the newsletter, or started a CLER application and stopped.
  • What people did. CLER applications started and submitted, event registrations, newsletter signups, donations by count and value, and resource downloads.
  • Where they came from, and how search is doing. Channels and top landing pages, with the pages losing people flagged, plus the queries and pages gaining or losing ground.
  • What changed. A short list of anything that moved past a threshold, so it's five lines to read rather than forty charts.

Where it could go next

Only the first of these is in Phase 1. The other three are just directions, and none of them are priced here.

IN PHASE 1

The weekly email. GA4 and Search Console, five conversions tracked, and high intent flagged.

IF USEFUL

Your email platform and event registration data join it, so a campaign is visible end to end.

IF USEFUL

A one-page monthly version for the board. Same numbers, different altitude, no extra work for your team.

IF IT EARNS IT

Looker Studio, built around the questions the weekly email shows you actually ask, whether that's for you or someone you bring in later.

That's why I'm not suggesting Looker Studio yet. A dashboard built before anyone knows which questions get asked usually goes unopened, and you'd be paying me to guess at it. The weekly email would tell us what belongs in one.

Your direction
  • Policy & Issues

Three things I'd want to talk through. Events and Circles side by side may confuse people, since a Circle is a recurring event, so the dropdown needs to say plainly what each one is. Connect needs a defined job, and I'd make it the home for finding a Circle, subscribing, getting in touch, and wherever Membership redirects once it sunsets. Who We Are needs a place in the new five, and Donate would do more as a persistent button than a menu item.

On the homepage

Timely content, above the fold Priority

A slot at the top for whatever matters most that month, editable by your team without a developer, with a fallback so it's never sitting empty.

Desktop typography & hierarchy Priority

Your words: the text is huge on desktop. When body copy sits close to headline size there's nothing left to signal what matters, so the page flattens out.

Pathways for each audience

Three clear routes off the first screen: a woman looking to join, a funder looking for impact, and a partner or journalist looking for evidence.

One obvious next action

Donate, Sign Up, Join/Renew and Apply currently compete on the same screen. Each route would get one main action, with the others stepping down a level.

Impact, made concrete

15,000 women, 48 states, 300+ CLER graduates. Those numbers are already on the site, but they're not doing much above the fold.

The women you serve, visible

Faces and named stories in the top third of the page, rather than only in a testimonial band near the footer.

The audience journey work sits with you and your strategy partner, and the final menu should come out of that rather than out of this document. My side is making sure whatever you land on is straightforward to build and to measure.

06 · Technical foundation

Technical foundation

The technical work, running alongside everything else.

Tracking, rebuilt properly Priority

Both G-TYN0VYM5DG and GTM-NF859QGV fire today, with Site Kit running alongside them. GTM becomes the single source, the duplicate path is retired, five conversions get defined and set up, and the Meta Pixel goes in through GTM with your cookie consent plugin wired to Consent Mode v2.

The legacy page clearance Priority

Tag archives and dead events come out of the sitemaps as a configuration change, then the rest is a bulk tidy: 180 existing redirects checked, 90 soft 404s fixed or removed, and 89 genuine 404s left to 404 properly.

Performance Priority

246KB of homepage HTML, 20 stylesheets and scripts, and an Instagram embed loading on first paint. Defer what isn't needed, lazy-load below the fold, serve modern image formats, and measure against real Core Web Vitals data rather than a lab score.

Search Console & the anchor links

233 of your top pages are table-of-contents anchors like #toc-introduction. Between them they have 13.4m impressions and 291 clicks, which is 27% of everything you see in Search Console. Fixing it also makes every average readable.

Schema & answer engines

Structured data on the pages in this phase: organisation, programs, events and FAQ. It's what helps search engines and AI assistants quote you accurately, and given where your traffic comes from it's good value for the effort.

Templates, type scale & accessibility

One global header and footer, one type scale, one-off page styles cleared as pages are rebuilt, and a pass on contrast, focus states, heading order and alt text while we're in there.

On builders, I'd suggest Elementor.

Templates only work inside the builder they were made in, and you've got three ways of building a page at the moment. Elementor builds more cleanly and your new Events subdomain is already heading that way. Nothing gets re-platformed: your Divi pages carry on working, and pages move across only as they're worked on anyway. Happy to build in Divi if you'd rather, and I'll confirm once I've seen the Events build.

One thing worth just switching on, with no involvement from me: WP Engine's managed updates take a backup, apply the update, and roll it back automatically if something breaks. A few dollars a month, and it makes plugin updates far less of a worry.
