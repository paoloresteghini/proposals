01

The page backlog is three things, which makes it fixable

Your SEO plugin submits a sitemap entry for every tag you've ever used (1,133) and every event you've ever held (1,157, back to a Governor Rauner day). In a 1,000 page sample of the worst bucket, 92% were events, tags and recaps, and not one had ever been crawled. So it's a configuration change and a bulk tidy, not four thousand decisions. The member portal posts you described weren't in that sample; the URL export finds those.

02

The decline started in January, and it isn't a penalty

You have no manual actions, and your average position improved, 17.6 to 12.1 year on year. Impressions are down 42% on the same month last year and clicks 22%, so it isn't only seasonal. Your traffic sits almost entirely in definitional questions, the kind AI answers have been absorbing, though I'd want to check that rather than assert it.

03

Almost none of that traffic reaches what you care about

Over sixteen months, search sent 106,766 clicks to policy briefs, 638 to CLER, 434 to events, 20 to membership and 13 to Donate. That's 167 times more people arriving at a brief than at CLER, about 6,673 a month. Socialism questions alone account for around 60% of your search visibility. The briefs bring women to the site beautifully, and nothing is asked of them once they arrive.

That last one is the biggest opportunity here. I've not put a projected number on it, because I'd be guessing at a conversion rate until it's measured. That's what month one is for.

03 · Measurement & reporting

Measurement & reporting

Reporting you don't have to go looking for.

You asked what should be in it and where it goes from here.

What I'd suggest: a custom weekly email.

Built for you rather than taken off a shelf, with AI doing the reading. It takes your analytics and Search Console, works out what actually changed, and writes it up in plain English every Monday. Nothing to log into or learn. It's the smaller of the two options we discussed, and right for now for the reason you gave: nobody in-house has a digital marketing background, so what helps most is findings you can read. Anyone you hire later inherits it with history behind it.

What would be in it

  • Who's showing real interest. Not visits, but sequences: read a brief then joined the newsletter, or started a CLER application and stopped.
  • What people did. CLER applications started and submitted, event registrations, newsletter signups, donations by count and value, downloads.
  • Where they came from. Channels and top landing pages, the pages losing people flagged, plus queries gaining or losing ground.
  • What changed. Anything past a threshold, so it's five lines rather than forty charts.

Where it could go next

IN PHASE 1

The weekly email. GA4 and Search Console, five conversions, high intent flagged.

IF USEFUL

Your email platform and event registrations join it, so a campaign is visible end to end.

IF USEFUL

A one-page monthly version for the board. Same numbers, different altitude.

IF IT EARNS IT

Looker Studio, around the questions the weekly email shows you actually ask.

Only the first is in Phase 1; the rest are directions, not priced here. A dashboard built before anyone knows which questions get asked usually goes unopened, and you'd be paying me to guess. The weekly email tells us what belongs in one.

06 · Technical foundation

Technical foundation

The technical work, running alongside everything else.

Tracking, rebuilt properly Priority

Both G-TYN0VYM5DG and GTM-NF859QGV fire today, with Site Kit alongside. GTM becomes the single source, the duplicate retired, five conversions set up, the Meta Pixel in with consent wired to Consent Mode v2.

The legacy page clearance Priority

Tag archives and dead events come out of the sitemaps as a configuration change. The rest is a bulk tidy: 180 redirects checked, 90 soft 404s fixed, 89 real 404s left alone.

Performance Priority

246KB of homepage HTML, 20 stylesheets and scripts, an Instagram embed on first paint. Defer, lazy-load, modern image formats, measured against real Core Web Vitals rather than a lab score.

Search Console & the anchor links

233 of your top pages are anchors like #toc-introduction, carrying 13.4m impressions and 291 clicks. That's 27% of what you see in Search Console, and fixing it makes every average readable.

Schema & answer engines

Structured data on the pages in this phase: organization, programs, events and FAQ. It helps search engines and AI assistants quote you accurately, which given your traffic is good value.

Templates, type scale & accessibility

One global header and footer, one type scale, one-off styles cleared as pages are rebuilt, plus contrast, focus states, heading order and alt text.

On builders, I'd suggest Elementor.

Templates only work inside the builder they were made in, and you've got three ways of building a page today. Elementor builds more cleanly and your new Events subdomain is already heading that way. Nothing gets re-platformed: your Divi pages carry on working, and pages move across only as they're worked on. Happy to build in Divi if you'd rather.

One thing worth switching on without me: WP Engine's managed updates take a backup, apply the update, and roll back automatically if it breaks. A few dollars a month, and plugin updates stop being a worry.
